# C-INNING-001 research contract

The output is the initial defending team's final-game win probability under the frozen `minimal-pitch-service-v1` game-winner continuation. The evaluator stops on a change of the **current half-inning** or a game winner. A replacement difference is `100 × (substitute − keep)` percentage points. It is a separate `inning_end` result and is never added to the PA event components.

At each PA, the same fixed TRAIN repertoire-frequency pitch-type policy is used at all counts. The frozen `Engine.predict_counts` blend supplies legal pitch outcomes. Each terminal-event probability is the `solve_pa(...).baseline_values` result with that event's basis utility. The optimal `values` are never used for this distribution. The frozen empirical runner advancement propagates each terminal event; the frozen game-winner WE values boundary states. The pitcher remains fixed through the horizon; subsequent substitutions, steals, fatigue, and new batter profiles are not modeled.

Inputs for both keep and substitute must share the initial game state, lineup, prior-date batter profiles, model bundle, WE, advancement, policy definition, and caps. The lineup must be known before the decision. Recorded later hitters or outcomes are never used to fill missing future inputs. Unknown future hitters, unsupported pitchers, unverified eligibility, extra-inning initial states, probability errors, and caps produce an abstention or bounded result, not an invented point estimate. A dated active roster is only a screening set and does not establish actual managerial availability.

Every evaluated result carries `evaluation_identity`: frozen bundle manifest SHA (or an explicitly labeled synthetic provider), full initial state/count/date, hash of ordered lineup/profile inputs, config hash, policy ID, horizon, and initial defender. `compare` requires exact identity agreement and distinct pitcher IDs as well as verified candidate eligibility. Probability distributions from both the PA solver and runner advancement must have finite, nonnegative entries and unit mass within tolerance. Initial extra-inning states abstain; within a regulation half, the frozen advancement handles a resulting automatic runner or walkoff at the terminal boundary.

Deterministic mass is propagated by PA. Boundary mass receives frozen WE. Any frontier mass with no known hitter, insufficient mass for a model call, or a resource cap remains unresolved. The reported WE interval is `[absorbed_value, absorbed_value + unresolved_mass]`, without renormalization. The total absorbed plus unresolved mass must be one within the configured tolerance. A replacement interval, when possible, is `[substitute_lower − keep_upper, substitute_upper − keep_lower] × 100` percentage points. Point values require zero unresolved mass within tolerance.

The experiment config freezes caps and scope before the first real inference. Synthetic transition tests check mechanics; the 2025 C0 example exercises a supported real-state input but supplies only one hitter and cannot validate an actual replacement decision or whole-inning point value. A separately preregistered two-out amendment selects the earliest eligible PA start from A's already fixed six 2025 DEV games by date/game/PA/pitch key. It was committed before that result was read. The amendment likewise uses only the current hitter and leaves later hitter mass unresolved.
